# Round 10 decisions

- **track-d merged without its d5 review.** The owner asked for the merge and a release at once. The partial d5 run (keyframes 81f6f353, withers d4f932de..ef9ad9de, wind charges f142f9c1) stopped before its review. Track D's round 10 review uses base `r9-d5-base`, so it covers those commits too.
- **The relay needs no rebuild for protocol 6.** `iw4l-master` carries Minecraft messages as opaque packets and does not depend on `minecraft_net`. Its own protocol is `master_protocol::PROTOCOL_VERSION` 10, unchanged since e06114b0 (2026-10-11). The `iw4l-master.exe` in the game folder has the same SHA-256 as `D:\iw4l-target\release\iw4l-master.exe` from release 2. Its file date is 13 October because cargo did not relink an unchanged binary. Every release still builds it with `-p iw4l-master` and copies it in.
- **Five tracks at a time, set by disk.** D: had 27 GB free and C: 19 GB. A track's target dir is 6-8 GB, and 12-14 GB after a full suite. So at most five track target dirs exist at once, four on D: and one on `C:\iw4l-wt\`. Test binaries are deleted from a target after its merge's full suite. The two cargo slots are shared by all of them.
- **Phantoms go to the M3 mob track, not track E.** A phantom is a new mob family, so it uses D0's shared mob registry like the other remaining mobs. Track E keeps the other known limits.
- **PROTOCOL is bumped only at release time.** Tracks run in parallel and must not bump `minecraft_net::PROTOCOL` (several bumps would collide at merge, and some briefs say "bump to 7"; the run notes override that). A track that changes a message notes it under "## Protocol" in its log. Before a release build, if any merged commit since the last release changed a message, one commit bumps PROTOCOL by one (with its codec test asserts).
- **Tracks run in segments.** Each track-runner run is a few groups with its own review, so finished segments can merge for the daily releases and a stopped session loses at most one segment. Every run's args are saved in `docs/round10/runs/<segment>.json` before it starts (round 9 lost its launcher scripts with the session scratchpad).
- **Track D's first segment reviews round 9's d5 work.** Its review base is `r9-d5-base`, so the review also covers keyframes, withers and wind charges.
- **D0's shape: a static mob-family registry.** A new family is its own files plus one line in its track's own group file (entities, spawn rows, terrain). Each group owns a range of hit-key bytes so parallel tracks never collide: pests (D0) 36-39, trial (D) 40-43, deep_dark (W) 44-47, land (M1) 48-63, water (M2) 64-79, night (M3) 80-95, bosses (F) 96-99. D0 adds one generic arm at every shared site, and the tracks never edit those sites again. Existing families keep their code. Friends get a generic `MobShow.family` payload (flags and eight values). Silverfish and endermites are the proof family. The horse family (camels, camel husks, zombie and skeleton horses) stays in the cow store and goes to M3, the only track allowed to edit the old kind lists for it.
- **The mob split differs from round 9's batches.** Camels and camel husks moved to M3 (they share the cow store's horse kinds with the undead horses). Bees and sniffers moved to M1. The sulfur cube moved to M2 (it is bucketable). Silverfish and endermites moved to D0 as its proof.
- **First Elden Ring boss: Margit, the Fell Omen** (track F's design doc, `docs/ELDEN-RING-BOSSES.md`). He is humanoid, has two phases and a readable moveset, and fits an altar summon. His numbers are marked as estimates until real game data exists. He is saved as a `minecraft:marker` carrying fork data, which vanilla keeps and ignores, and loading resets the fight.
- **Lower load after the crash on 2026-10-28.** The PC hard-reset at 20:27 (Kernel-Power 41, no bugcheck, no low-memory event; the laptop has had seven earlier unexpected shutdowns since September) while six track runs and the brief workflows kept both cargo slots at `-j 8` for hours, with rustc kept off Windows power throttling. That points to heat or power under sustained all-core load, not memory. From then on the slot file is `slots=2`, `jobs=4`, `priority=low` (at most eight rustc threads, below normal priority), at most four track runs at once, and brief writers that never run cargo. Queued segments start when a running one ends. This replaces the "five tracks at a time" limit above.
- **The M briefs give every cross-track hook one owner.**
  - M1 owns `family_goal_step_with`, `animal_brain.rs`, `long_jump.rs`, `World::biome_in_tag`, the loot-context rewrite, `PlayerControls.sprinting`, `ClientFx.item_crumbs` and `TerrainFamily::shadow_radius`. If M2's fish-drawn needs the shadow hook first, it builds it under M1's exact name and subject, and the second track to merge drops its copy.
  - M2 owns the water hooks (`swim.rs`, `Breath`, `swim_sounds`, `quiet_steps`, `OWN_DYING_TRAVEL`) and the player-effect queue. M3 owns `walk_target` and `Family::thunder_hit`.
  - No track adds an ambient hook: families set `MobCore.ambient_interval` and voice it in their own tick.
- **M run order: M1 first, then M3, then M2.** m1a and m3a need only d0a; most M2 segments wait for M1 hooks (m2b for rabbits' `family_goal_step_with`, m2f and m2g for `animal_brain.rs`). Parrots, bees, allays and happy ghasts wait for d0b; allay note blocks and creaking hearts wait for W's w2. The orchestrator merges `minecraft-survival` into an M track before each segment. A group whose wait is not met moves to the next segment.
- **Small M brief points, settled the vanilla-like or smallest way:**
  - **Frog legs:** a frog keeping its legs still mid-jump stays left for later, because M3's `walk_target` gets no mob.
  - **`nautilus: false`:** M2 adds that one field to M3's horse literal at `mount_inventory.rs:53`.
  - **`Prey::BabiesOnLand`:** stays a logged hook commit.
  - **Root-file hooks:** D0-3 wins over D0-2.1's wording, so a mob track may add a hook to a root file in its own named commit.
  - **Visibility commits:** M2 keeps its two one-word visibility commits.
  - **Unchanged:** `undead-horses-live` stays one group, and the phantom spawner stays in `custom_spawners.rs`.
  - **Frogspawn anchor:** M1's brief now names M2's coral anchor for frogspawn in `tick_block`.
- **W checks D0's `Family::emits_sounds`.** It drops a family's whole movement emission, so W's runs check that the `EVENTS` mobs still post their step and swim game events (note in the w1 and w2 run specs).
- **Elden Ring is not installed on this PC** (no Steam install on C: or D:). Track F's proof of concept cannot read game files. It uses a stand-in model and our own data. `docs/ELDEN-RING-BOSSES.md` says what real game data would need later.
- **Track F builds on D: and merges f1 with f2.** C: had 13 GB, and f2 builds the whole game (6-8 GB), which would take C: below the 8 GB the page file needs. F's target moved to `D:\iw4l-wt\track-f` (the old one held only the 1 GB `elden_boss` build). Segment f1 (the pure `elden_boss` crate, nothing in game) is not merged on its own, because a full suite costs about 45 minutes of both slots: track-f takes `minecraft-survival` in (cea44a86, tag `r10-f2-base`) and f1 merges together with f2.
- **Idle targets are deleted after their merge.** The D0 and E1 targets were deleted after d0a and e1a merged (their next segments d0b and e1b rebuild), so M1 and F fit on D:.
- **D's merge kept both sides everywhere.** The d1 merge (1a893d11) had 14 conflict hunks. `PlayerView` keeps E1's `vehicle_health` and D's `last_death`. `item_tint::of_stack` keys by D's `dials::stack_key` through E1's `of_stack_at`, which also carries the dye colour, and the held item uses `of_stack_at(dials.key(&stack_key(stack)), stack)`. The particle textures list both tracks' sprites. `spawner_may_spawn` keeps D's `SpawnerReason`, D0's `Rules::Family` arm and `Walk::MonsterOver`. D0's family spawn tests pass `SpawnerReason::Spawner`. A family's spawn rule is not skipped for trial spawners: the family's own rule decides.
- **E2's merge kept both sides and gave the shared hit list the registry's mobs.** The e2a merge had 14 conflict hunks with D, D0 and F. E2 replaced `arrow_targets`' walk over every store with `EntityWorld::hit_boxes()`, which thrown things share; the merge adds the registry families (silverfish, endermites, Margit) to `hit_boxes`, so arrows still hit them and thrown things now do too. `PlayerHitKind::Fireworks` keeps E2's `by` next to D's `Typed` hit. Mob-thrown things and mob wind charges keep D's `MOB_THROWER` guard, with E2's hurt that draws no seed. Harming's undead check is E2's `entity_type` test or the family's `undead` trait. Left for D's d2 merge: `hurt_for_player` still hands a registry family `minecraft:player_attack` for thrown hits, so the breeze must learn the real damage type there and skip its anger on wind charges (E2's `NO_ANGER_FROM_WIND_CHARGE`).
