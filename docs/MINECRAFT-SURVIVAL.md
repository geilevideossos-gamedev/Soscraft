# Minecraft survival: state, limitations, testing

State of branch `minecraft-survival` after round 7 (2026-10-10). It is ahead of upstream `main` (f608f85) and 0 behind. Rules, goal and roadmap: [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

Round 7 finished the single-player Minecraft side: the crate split, saving everything vanilla saves, the dimension switch, the Nether and the End with the dragon fight. Multiplayer is next. The round's plan and track logs are in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md) and `docs/round7/`.

## Implemented

Most of this was added by the fork. Items marked [upstream] were already in upstream's README.

### World and rendering
- [upstream] Minecraft 26.3 world generation from MinecraftOSS, with day and night, sky, clouds, lighting, block, mob and footstep sounds, and a minimap.
- Back-face culling like vanilla, so cross plants no longer flicker.
- Faces are culled by vanilla occlusion shapes (`face_culling.rs`), blocks turned about X (horizontal logs, upside-down stairs, ceiling vines) included. Cactus, spawners, ice and beacons no longer leave holes in their neighbours.
- Vanilla `BlockColors` tinting. Faces are tinted only where vanilla has a tint source: grass, foliage per leaf type, stems, lily pad, leaf litter, water cauldron. The stonecutter saw stays grey.
- A translucent pass for stained glass and panes, ice, honey, slime, tinted glass and the nether portal. Fluids are only hidden by faces that actually cover them, and clouds are ordered against blended terrain.
- Translucent faces are drawn farthest first within each section, as vanilla sorts them (`MeshData.sortQuads` by the distance to each quad's centre): when the section is built, and again when the camera moves to another block for sections within 2 sections of it or seen straight along an axis, or when it passes to another side of a farther section (`LevelRenderer.scheduleTranslucentSectionResort`). Only the index buffer is rewritten, and a worst case of 50 water sections costs about 0.4 ms each time the camera enters a new block.
- Animated textures from `.mcmeta` (fire, lava, water, portal, sea lantern, prismarine, sculk, stonecutter and more), in the world and in icons.
- The break-crack overlay follows the block's real model.
- Model face textures turn like vanilla's `FaceBakery`: a face's `rotation` turns its texture by quarters (horizontal logs, doors, pistons, anvils), and `uvlock` keeps the texture locked to the world for every blockstate turn, quarter turns included (stair tops, fences, walls, glass panes), in the world, on dropped and held blocks and in icons.
- Block-entity models: chests (double, trapped, ender, copper), shulker boxes, banners, heads, bell, conduit, decorated pot and books. They are used in the world, as icons, as dropped items and as held items.
- Falling blocks and primed TNT are meshed like terrain, with no flicker.
- Water like vanilla: seagrass, kelp, bubble columns and waterlogged blocks hold water, there are no faces between water cells, and water is single-sided apart from the vanilla backward surface.
- XP orbs and the burning overlay with two flames.
- Weather from `IW4L_MINECRAFT_WEATHER`: rain darkens the sky and puts out fire.
- A render error no longer quits the game. Validation and internal GPU errors drop only the broken frame and are written to the game log as `render error ignored`, at most once every 5 s with a count of the ones in between. Running out of GPU memory quits only after 30 times in 10 s, and a lost GPU device still quits; the world is saved on the way out.
- Many explosions at once no longer overflow a render index buffer. Model, spark, effect, decal and glass streams are uploaded every frame, sun shadows of models are drawn only from what the GPU holds, and every MW2 world, model and effect draw in the colour and sun-shadow passes is checked against its bound index buffer before it is sent. A skipped draw is written to the game log as `skipped a ... draw`, naming the pass and stream, for the first 8 times and then every 600th.
- With the eyes in lava, vanilla's lava fog (`LavaFogEnvironment`) closes in: colour #991A00, from 0.25 to 1 block (0 to 5 with Fire Resistance, -8 to half the render distance for spectators), no sky, sun or clouds.
- Rain splashes and sounds like vanilla (`ClientLevel.tickWeatherEffects` with the default weather radius 10): up to 99 `rain` splashes a tick (0.225 x 21 x 21 x rain level squared) on the top motion-blocking block of random columns within 10 blocks of the eyes and 10 blocks up or down, only where the biome rains (has precipitation and is warm enough at that height), on the block's collision top or fluid surface; over lava, magma and lit campfires they are `smoke`. Every 1 to 3 ticks `weather.rain` (volume 0.2) plays at the last splash, or the muffled `weather.rain.above` (volume 0.1, pitch 0.5) when it lands on a roof above the camera.
- A tool that breaks in the player's hands (flint and steel, a blocking shield) bursts into 5 of its item particles in front of the eyes with its break sound (`LivingEntity.breakItem`). Bone meal that grows something sparkles with green `happy_villager` stars (level event 1505, with its `item.bone_meal.use` sound): 15 inside a plant, up to its vanilla shape height, or 45 tries over the ground 3 blocks around grass, nylium, moss and netherrack and in water; rooted dirt and mangrove leaves sparkle in the block below. Lava fizzes (`block.lava.extinguish` and 8 `large_smoke` over the block, level event 1501) where water hardens it into obsidian, cobblestone or stone, where it turns to basalt and where it flows over a block and destroys it; water poured in the Nether steams with 8 `large_smoke`. A campfire the player douses with a shovel or a water bucket bursts into 20 of its smoke puffs and 20 low `smoke` puffs (`CampfireBlock.douse` on the client); one doused by a splash potion only hisses, and fire put out makes only its sound, as in vanilla.
- Primed TNT smokes from its top every tick. Minecraft explosions (TNT, creepers, ghast fireballs, beds and respawn anchors) flash like vanilla: a big one (radius 2 or more that breaks blocks) seeds 48 `explosion` flashes over 8 ticks, a small or harmless one a single flash, and the next tick scatters up to 512 `poof` and `smoke` puffs through the air the blast reached, one for each block position it picked (air included, as `ServerExplosion` counts them). MW2 blasts keep their own MW2 effects.
- The display tick animates nether portals and plays the ambient sounds of fire, soul fire, lava under air (`block.lava.pop` and `block.lava.ambient`) and lit campfires (`block.campfire.crackle`) at vanilla's chances, volumes and pitches. Fire smokes (`large_smoke`, three puffs a tick on the ground, two against each burning side), lava under air spits an ember with each pop and lit campfires one time in five (`lava` particles, which leap, fall, glow at full block light and trail `smoke`).
- Third person: while a Minecraft block or item (or an empty hand) is selected the MW2 body puts its gun away and holds the selected item in its right hand and the offhand item in its left, as vanilla's `thirdperson_righthand` and `thirdperson_lefthand` displays place them, and worn armour is drawn on it, bending at the elbows and knees, with vanilla's armour glint (`enchanted_glint_armor`, on the first layer only, as `EquipmentLayerRenderer` does) when enchanted. While the inventory is open the body leaves the world for the inventory's character box, which wears the same gear.

### Movement on blocks (MW2 movement with vanilla rules)
- Ground, falls and footsteps. Body size is 0.6 x 1.8 blocks, or 1.5 crouched. Prone keeps MW2's height.
- Swimming: sink, rise, dive, sprint-swim, hop out at ledges. Lava movement.
- Climbing ladders, vines and scaffolding. Sneaking stops at edges.
- Slowdown in berry bushes and cobwebs. Using an item slows you to 0.2.
- A player is never embedded in blocks; anyone inside a block is pushed out, except a spectator.
- Creative flight like vanilla (`LocalPlayer.aiStep`, `Player.travel`): a second jump press (Space) within 7 ticks (350 ms) of the first starts or stops flying, so a double tap from the ground jumps and then flies. While flying, Space rises and the crouch and prone keys (C and Ctrl) sink, at up to 7.5 blocks/s; the player stands up, and horizontal speed builds up to about 10.9 blocks/s, or twice that with the sprint key (Shift) held while moving forward. Flying has no gravity and no water or lava movement, cobwebs and berry bushes still slow it, and sinking onto the ground ends the flight. A fall only counts from where the flight ended. Flight lives in the MW2 player state and command, so prediction on the host stays in step. Spectators are always flying and never land.
- Spectators fly through blocks like vanilla (`noPhysics`): at the same speeds, straight through walls, the ground and fluids, with no cobweb or berry bush slowdown and nothing pushing them out of a block. Other players walk through them. A player who leaves spectator inside a block is pushed out of it.

### Survival and combat
- Hearts, hunger, saturation, exhaustion, regeneration and starvation. MW2 health follows the hearts at 5 per point in every life. MW2's own regeneration is off, and the MW2 scripts can't heal a live player or change its maximum health, so a grenade or bullet costs the same hearts after every respawn.
- Vanilla fall damage, including the blocks that soften falls. Drowning with air bubbles.
- Burning from lava, fire, soul fire, magma and campfires, and putting it out.
- XP levels, the XP bar and vanilla XP from blocks.
- Armour enchantments protect like vanilla, read from the jar's enchantment `damage_protection` effects and damage type tags: each worn piece adds its points (Protection 1 a level against everything but `/kill` and the void, Fire Protection 2 against fire and lava, Blast Protection 2 against explosions, Projectile Protection 2 against arrows and fireballs, Feather Falling 3 against falls and ender pearls), and the total, capped at 20, takes 4% a point off the hit after armour and Resistance (`CombatRules.getDamageAfterMagicAbsorb`); starvation bypasses it. Fire Protection also shortens every new burn by 15% a level (`burning_time`). Frost Walker boots (read from the enchantment's `location_changed` `replace_disk` and `damage_immunity` effects) freeze still water with air above into frosted ice in a disk of radius 2 plus the level around the feet each time the feet enter a new block on the ground, skipping cells a body stands in, and spare the player magma and campfire burns. Frosted ice ages and melts back into water like vanilla (`FrostedIceBlock`): every placement or age change waits 60 to 120 ticks, it ages in light brighter than 11 minus its age when it has fewer than four frosted neighbours or one time in three, and a frosted block left with fewer than two frosted neighbours melts at once. An enchantment only counts in the slots it names (Frost Walker on the feet).
- Vanilla attack cooldown, crits, sweeps and knockback. Shield blocking and the totem of undying. A raised shield takes the whole hit from the front 90 degrees (26.3's shield `blocks_attacks` reduces it by base 0 plus factor 1), and a fully blocked hit knocks nobody back, neither the player nor the attacker (`LivingEntity.blockedByItem` only pushes the blocker after a partial block). While the player eats, drinks, blocks or charges a trident they move at a fifth of their speed and can't sprint, 26.3's default `use_effects`; only spears differ, and they have no use yet.
- Vanilla death messages and a Killed By card. A Minecraft death is not counted as an MW2 suicide. Every Minecraft death plays `entity.player.death` (volume 1, voice pitch 0.9 to 1.1) in place of the hurt sound of the blow that killed, so lava, fire, fall and drowning deaths are heard as vanilla does.
- Dying drops the 36 inventory slots, then the offhand, armour, body and saddle slots, at the death spot like vanilla: from 0.3 below the eye, with vanilla's random spread and a 2 s pickup delay. Crafting grid, bench inputs and the cursor stack go with them. Items with Curse of Vanishing are destroyed (a cursed enchanted book still drops). MW2 guns stay in their hotbar slots. Experience orbs worth 7 points per level, at most 100, drop at the feet, and the XP level starts again at 0. Only a death drops. A match end, MW2 spectating and the suicide MW2 does when a live player picks another team or Spectator in the menu drop nothing, and that team-change suicide keeps the XP level. A player in spectator mode drops nothing and no XP when killed, and keeps the items and XP level, as in vanilla (`ServerPlayer.die` and `restoreFrom`).
- The `keep_inventory` game rule (off by default, saved in `game_rules.dat`) keeps the items, the XP level and its progress through a death, as vanilla does. Hunger and health still reset.
- The world difficulty (Normal for a new world, changed with `/difficulty` and saved in `level.dat`) works like vanilla: the server thread gets it for mob spawning, despawning and behaviour (Peaceful removes hostile mobs), hunger and starvation follow it (Peaceful regenerates and never starves, Easy starves down to 10 health, Normal to 1, Hard to death), and mob hits on the player scale with it: none on Peaceful, half plus one (at most the full hit) on Easy, the full hit on Normal and x1.5 on Hard.
- `/kill` kills like vanilla `genericKill`: the health goes to 0 through armour, Resistance and absorption, a totem of undying doesn't save the player, and the death message is `<name> was killed`.
- The void kills like vanilla `fellOutOfWorld` (`Entity.checkBelowWorld`, `LivingEntity.onBelowWorld`): a player whose feet are more than 64 blocks below the world's lowest block (below Y -128 in the overworld; the floor comes from the dimension's `min_y`) takes 4 damage every tick, which lands every 10 ticks through the hurt cooldown. It goes through armour, Resistance and game-mode invulnerability, so creative and spectator players die in it too; absorption still takes it, and a totem of undying doesn't save the player. The death message is `<name> fell out of the world`, or `<name> didn't want to live in the same world as <mob>` within 5 s of a mob's hit, and void damage counts as the highest fall for a fall death right after it, as in vanilla.
- When the `show_death_messages` game rule is on (the default), the death message goes to the chat, like vanilla, and stays readable while dead. A Minecraft death never goes to the MW2 kill feed on the left, neither as a text line nor as a skull obituary; the Killed By card and the death animation stay. A death an MW2 player is credited with, such as a frag, keeps its normal kill feed line, and MW2 maps are unchanged.
- Game modes: survival, creative, adventure and spectator, with vanilla's abilities for each (`GameType.updatePlayerAbilities`). `/gamemode` switches it, and each player's mode is saved in their player file. A new player starts in the world's default `GameType` from `level.dat` (survival for a new world); `IW4L_MINECRAFT_GAMEMODE` switches to another mode on load for testing. Creative and spectator players are invulnerable like vanilla: no MW2 bullets, blasts, knife or killstreak damage, no mob hits, falls, lava, fire, cactus or berry bushes, drowning, starvation, poison or harming, and they never get hungry. Lava sets them alight for one tick only, so the flames show only while they stand in it. `/kill` and the void still kill them. Mobs don't target or attack them, and spectators pick up no items or XP. In creative and spectator the hearts, armour, hunger, air and XP bar are hidden; the hotbar stays in creative. Creative players can eat on a full stomach from either hand without using up the food.
- Building by game mode, like vanilla (`MultiPlayerGameMode`, `ServerPlayerGameMode.destroyBlock`, `Player.blockActionRestricted`):
  - Creative players break the targeted block at once on a click, and again every 6 ticks while the button is held. Bedrock and other unbreakable blocks break too; swords, tridents and maces break nothing. Creative reach is 5 blocks for blocks and mobs (4.5 and 3 otherwise), and a click into the air has no miss delay.
  - A block a creative player breaks drops no items and no experience, wears no tool and leaves no water where ice was. A filled shulker box still drops with its contents, and chest and furnace contents still spill. MW2 bullets still mine by hardness in creative, but what they break drops nothing either. Explosions still drop like in survival.
  - Creative players use up nothing: placed blocks, doors, scaffolding, planted berries, thrown snowballs, eggs and ender pearls, spawn eggs, food put on a campfire, fire charges and bone meal stay in the hotbar, and flint and steel doesn't wear. The ender pearl cooldown stays.
  - Adventure and spectator players can't build: no hand mining, no placing or planting, and no buckets, flint and steel, fire charges, bone meal or spawn eggs on blocks. While the player is in adventure or spectator, MW2 bullets mine nothing; explosions still blow craters. Adventure players still open doors, chests and buttons, eat, drink, block with a shield, throw and fight. Spectators can't use or hit anything with a Minecraft item.
- Spectators can't touch anything, like vanilla: no hotbar and no hand or gun in view, E opens nothing, and the hotbar number keys, mouse wheel, drop (Q) and swap-hands (F) keys do nothing. MW2 guns don't fire or aim, grenades and the knife don't work, use and reload do nothing and the weapon can't be switched. Lava, fire, magma, cactus, berry bushes, cobwebs and the rest of the blocks a body touches leave them alone, a fire from before goes out at once, and they make no step or fall sounds. Mobs ignore them and they pick up nothing (see game modes above). Chat, commands, the perspective key (F5) and the minimap (M) still work.
- MW2 explosives (frag, semtex, C4, claymore, RPG, grenade launcher, killstreak blasts) hurt and push mobs, and a player's kills drop loot and XP. Tactical grenades (concussion, flash and smoke) leave no crater. Every other MW2 blast does, including killstreak bombs (airstrike, stealth bomber) and any other script blast meant as an explosion, splash or artillery hit. A crater is a vanilla explosion on the Minecraft server with power radius / 72 blocks, capped at 8 (a frag about 3.6, TNT 4): blocks break by vanilla blast resistance, so obsidian, water and lava hold and bedrock stays. Each blasted block drops its empty-hand loot with a chance of one in the power (stone gives cobblestone, ores their items), merged into stacks of up to 16, and gives no block XP. TNT a crater reaches is primed, and dropped items in it are hurt and pushed. Craters are saved with the world.
- A stun (concussion) grenade that hurts a mob slows it like a stunned MW2 player: Slowness IV (0.4x walk speed) for 2 s at the 512-unit edge up to 6 s at point blank. Flash grenades don't slow mobs, as they don't slow players.

### Mining and placing
- [upstream] Shooting and knifing breaks blocks by hardness, broken blocks drop items, and grenades blow craters.
- The hand ray follows the MW2 crosshair in every stance. Items that are not blocks are never placed.
- Torches (also soul, copper and redstone) hang on walls when a side face is clicked, and pop off when their support goes.
- Vanilla placement states: beds, banners, signs, heads, bells, lecterns, pots, shulker boxes, ladders, vines (one face per use, added to a vine already there), levers, buttons, lanterns, tripwire hooks, shelf mushrooms and glow lichen (the first looking direction with a support), lily pads and frogspawn (on the water source the crosshair meets), pointed dripstone (hanging when looking up, standing when looking down, the other way round where only that side has a support; it merges with an opposite tip unless sneaking, and a column thickens from tip to frustum, middle and base as tips are added). Farmland and dirt paths placed under a solid block become dirt (`FarmlandBlock` and `PathBlock.getStateForPlacement`), except under crops, stems and fence gates for farmland and fence gates for paths. Chests join into double chests.
- Blocks are only placed where vanilla `canSurvive` allows, on the client and again on the server: sugar cane needs water, a waterlogged block or frosted ice beside its ground, cactus a free side, flowers and saplings soil, torches a floor that holds their centre, carpets and cakes something below, rails a rigid top. A refused block stays in the hotbar; one the server refuses comes back as its item.
- Doors, trapdoors, fence gates, levers, buttons and note blocks.
- Scaffolding. Blocks that lose support break and drop. Sand and gravel fall. Sugar cane and cactus columns break one block a tick, corals, cakes and shelf mushrooms go with their support, stalagmites break and stalactites fall.
- Ice broken by hand, bullet or knife over a block in `#blocks_motion` or a liquid turns into a water source, except where water evaporates; blasts and Silk Touch leave none. Packed and blue ice never melt.
- Hand mining with Silk Touch or Fortune applies the enchantment to the drops, and Silk Touch gives no block experience.

### Crafting, inventory, containers
- 2x2 and 3x3 crafting from the 26.3 recipe data, with remainders and real stack sizes.
- The vanilla inventory, hotbar, status bars and station screens, drawn from the runtime packs.
- Vanilla clicks: drag-split, double-click, shift-click, number keys, the drop key (Q) with or without Ctrl, the swap-hands key (F) for the offhand, click outside to drop. Controller slot focus.
- Chests, barrels, furnace, smoker and blast furnace (they cook while closed), brewing stand, stonecutter, smithing table, grindstone, anvil and enchanting table.
- Structure loot tables, shulker boxes that keep their contents, a shared ender chest, and the offhand slot.
- Tooltips with MW2 gun stats and Minecraft tool stats.
- Enchanted items shimmer with the vanilla glint in the hotbar, the inventory, every container and station screen, on the cursor, in the first-person hand (main hand and offhand) and as dropped items on the ground, every copy of a dropped stack included. The world glint is added after the light, so it still shows in the dark, and fog covers it. Placed blocks never glint. As in vanilla, so do enchanted books, enchanted golden apples, bottles o' enchanting, written books, nether stars, end crystals, lodestone compasses and items with a glint override, but not stacks that only hold stored enchantments.
- Creative players get vanilla's creative inventory (`CreativeModeInventoryScreen`): the tabs read from the jar (with every potion, enchanted book and variant), the search tab, the inventory tab with armour, offhand and the trash slot, scrolling, and vanilla's click rules. Every slot change goes through the server's `set_slot` checks (`minecraft_survival/src/creative.rs`). The Operator Items tab follows the `minecraft_operator_items` option and level 2. C plus a number key saves the hotbar and X plus a number key loads it, in `iw4l-artifacts\hotbar.nbt` (vanilla's layout, per game install, not per world), shown in the Saved Hotbars tab.

### Items
- Eating, drinking and potions, with the vanilla first-person eat and drink animation, crumbs, sounds and burp. Food only counts when finished; letting go cancels. Glass bottles, buckets (water, lava, powder snow), cauldrons and waterlogging.
- Flint and steel and fire charges light fire, campfires and candles, and prime TNT.
- Hoes, shovels and axes change the block they are used on, read from the jar's 26.3 `block_transformer` data (`hoe`, `shovel` and `axe`, for the `#hoes`, `#shovels` and `#axes` items): a hoe tills grass, dirt and dirt paths into farmland and coarse dirt into dirt, a shovel flattens grass, dirt, podzol, mycelium, coarse and rooted dirt into a path (both only with air above and not from below), and an axe strips logs and wood (keeping their axis), scrapes a weathering stage off copper blocks, slabs, stairs, trapdoors, bars, grates, bulbs, lanterns, chains, lightning rods and statues, and takes the wax off waxed ones. Each use costs 1 durability (none in creative) and plays its sound; with a shield in the offhand the main hand only transforms while sneaking, as in vanilla.
- Snowballs, eggs, ender pearls, bottles o' enchanting, splash and lingering potions and wind charges are thrown with right-click and drawn in flight; potions splash their effects on mobs and players, and a wind charge burst launches the player without the fall damage of the jump. Tridents charge while right-click is held, are thrown on release, drawn in 3D in flight and in the hands, and come back with Loyalty; a Riptide one launches the player in water or rain. Sweet and glow berries.
- Campfire cooking: 4 slots, 30 s per item, nothing cooks while unlit. A placed campfire faces the way the player looks, is lit unless placed in water and is a signal fire over a hay bale, as vanilla places it. A shovel (`#douses_campfires`) puts a lit campfire out with a hiss (`block.fire.extinguish`) and loses 1 durability, unless the player sneaks (`CampfireBlock.useItemOn`); a water bucket waterlogs and douses it.
- Lit campfires send up their smoke (`campfire_cosy_smoke`, or the taller `campfire_signal_smoke` over hay: big translucent puffs that drift up for 4 to 6 or 14 to 16 seconds and fade out over the last 3), and food on them smokes from its corner. Every lit campfire in the loaded chunks smokes, as vanilla ticks all their block entities.
- Spawn eggs for every simulated mob.
- Dropped items burn up in fire, soul fire and lava, and lava sizzles. Netherite gear and ancient debris never burn and float on lava. Lit campfires leave items alone, as in vanilla.

### World simulation (integrated server thread)
- Fluids flow, and lava meeting water hardens. Fire spreads. TNT explodes.
- Nether portals: a 2-21 x 3-21 obsidian frame lights, shimmers with particles and hum, and collapses when the frame breaks.
- Simulated mobs: cow, mooshroom, horse, donkey, pig, chicken, sheep, zombie, husk, zombie villager, skeleton, stray, bogged, parched, creeper, spider, slime, enderman, witch, iron golem, wolf, bat and villager, the Nether mobs below, and the ender dragon, end crystals, shulkers and shulker bullets. Other kinds stay dormant.

### MW2 integration and UI
- [upstream] Guns sit in the hotbar with unlimited ammo. Skate 3 works on block collision.
- The Minecraft keys are their own binding layer beside the MW2 one, so a key can hold one of each. The vanilla defaults are E inventory, Q drop, F swap hands, T chat, / (the physical slash key) command and F5 perspective. The MW2 lethal grenade (frag, semtex, C4 or claymore) is on G, as in retail MW2, and the tactical one on B, so F only swaps hands. A settings file from before (header v1 to v3) whose lethal is exactly F and tactical exactly G, with B free, gets the new keys once and is saved as v4; any other choice is kept. Both grenade rows are on the Actions page of the controls menu. A gun never goes to the offhand, so with a gun selected the swap-hands key uses the offhand item while held instead: food is eaten, a shield raised, a block placed. The tactical key always throws. On a controller D-pad down is the swap-hands key, held with a gun selected it uses the offhand item the same way, and the tactical button always throws. A grenade thrown with a Minecraft item or an empty hand selected shows MW2's hands and grenade from the moment the grenade comes up until it is thrown, with the Minecraft item hidden meanwhile and the empty hand in MW2's own hand position. On the Minecraft map a Minecraft key takes the key from its MW2 binding, except while a use hint shows and the key is bound to MW2 use (+activate or +usereload) with no Minecraft screen open: then only the MW2 use fires, so E picks up a gun instead of opening the inventory. A key that also holds an MW2 grenade (`+frag` or `+smoke`) keeps its throw whatever is in hand, a gun, a Minecraft item or an empty hand, and its Minecraft action works only while a Minecraft screen is open. Off the Minecraft map the Minecraft keys do nothing. The minimap key M, the hotbar number keys and the controller D-pad stay fixed.
- The perspective key (F5) cycles the view like vanilla: first person, third person from behind, third person from the front, and back to first person. It works in the world but not while a Minecraft screen is open, and the view is kept for the session, through deaths and matches, but not saved. The third-person camera sits 4 blocks from the eye, behind it or in front of it looking back at the face, and the vanilla eight corner rays pull it in where a block is in the way, so it never looks inside a block. The MW2 character model is drawn at the predicted player position; the crosshair, the MW2 gun and hands, the first-person Minecraft item and the fire overlay are hidden, and the world is culled from the real camera. The block outline, mining and placing still follow the eyes, as in vanilla. Skating, the MW2 death camera, the killcam and the predator missile take the camera over as before.
- The chat key (T) opens the vanilla chat with an empty input line, and the command key (/) opens it with `/` already typed; while the command key has a binding, a typed `/` from any other key, such as Shift+7 on a German keyboard, also opens it with `/`. A pressed chat or command key wins over the typed `/`, so a chat key moved to / opens the chat empty. The key that opens it is never typed into it. Enter sends the line and closes the chat, Esc closes it without sending (and never opens the MW2 pause menu), Up and Down walk the last 100 sent lines, Page Up, Page Down and the mouse wheel (7 lines a notch, 1 with Shift) scroll, and Backspace, Delete, the arrows, Home and End edit the line, with Ctrl for whole words. Shift with the arrows, Home or End selects text, and Ctrl+A selects the whole line, like the vanilla edit box: the selection shows as a light box with blue text, typing replaces it, Backspace and Delete remove it, and an arrow without Shift drops it and steps from the cursor. Ctrl+C copies the selection to the system clipboard, Ctrl+X cuts it and Ctrl+V pastes over it, with Shift and Alt up as vanilla wants; copying or cutting nothing leaves the clipboard alone, and a paste loses `§`, line breaks, tabs and other control characters and stops at the 256-character limit, so a command copied with its line break pastes as one line. While Ctrl is held without Alt a key types nothing, so AltGr still types `@` and the other AltGr characters. Each key uses the Ctrl, Shift and Alt state of the moment it was pressed, even when several keys arrive in one frame. The line holds at most 256 characters and loses `§` and control characters; a sent line is trimmed and its runs of spaces are collapsed like vanilla. While the chat is open the mouse is free and no MW2 binding, Minecraft key (inventory, drop, swap hands, perspective), hotbar number, mouse wheel slot change, minimap or skate toggle, console key, mining or item use fires. Messages show bottom left above the hotbar at the vanilla size: 320 pixels wide, word-wrapped, at most 100 lines kept, 10 lines shown and 20 while the chat is open, each on a half-black background. A line stays for 9 s and fades out over the last second, as in vanilla. Chat lines go through a small command server (`crates/minecraft_commands`), which stamps the sender and sends `<name> message` back. The chat is cleared when a Minecraft match starts or ends, and its sent-line history is kept for the session. Sent commands (lines starting with `/`) are also saved like vanilla's `command_history.txt`: the last 50, a repeat of the last one skipped, in `iw4l-artifacts/command_history.txt` next to `settings.cfg`, and they are the first lines Up walks back to after a restart. While the player is dead the rest of the Minecraft HUD goes, but the chat lines stay on screen and fade as usual, like vanilla's chat behind the death screen, so the death message can be read; the input line closes on death and can't be opened until respawn.
- Guns left in a crafting grid are not duplicated. Esc closes a Minecraft screen. The Skate toggle is blocked while a screen is open.
- Options: the Minecraft minimap (M), Show Blood, and an Advanced Video page that replaces the Video page. Options > Controls has a Minecraft row that opens a page with the six Minecraft keys under their vanilla names; click a row and press a key to rebind it. The mouse wheel can't take a Minecraft key, so scrolling while a row listens is ignored. The bindings are saved in `settings.cfg` (now `// IW4L user settings v3`, with lines like `bind F5 mc_perspective`), and `unbind <key>` clears both layers of a key. A v1 or v2 `settings.cfg` from an older build gets the vanilla Minecraft defaults on its first load; after that a Minecraft key left unbound stays unbound.
- F1 to F12 can be bound in the controls menu or with `bind`, and keep their binding in `settings.cfg` after a restart. Keys outside the bind key list, such as the numpad, Insert or the backquote key, still lose their binding on a restart.
- The Minecraft world runs the level script and map entities of `mp_rust`, the map it stands in for, without that map's two kill triggers. Its `trigger_hurt` (10000 damage, brush model 15, MW2 z from -3040 to -864 around the map origin) made anyone suicide whose feet were 24 or more blocks below the spawn's Y, down to about 86 blocks below, within about 90 blocks west and 107 east, 110 north and 71 south of the spawn, in every game mode, with a Killed By card naming the player. Its `killTrigger` pocket (radius 40, height 30 at MW2 1080 1645 -186) did the same about 30 blocks east, 46 north and 5 below the spawn. Both are left out on the Minecraft map; MW2 maps keep them.
- When the world loads, the loading screen stays up like vanilla's until the 7x7 spawn chunks have collision and the camera's section is drawn (0.5 s close delay, 30 s timeout). Every MW2 spawn and respawn lands on the Minecraft spawn, or on the nearest free dry column within 10 blocks when someone stands there. Any player whose chunk has no collision yet waits in place until it has, in that area or anywhere else (after a `/tp`, for example).

### Commands
- Commands typed in the chat with `/` run on the command server with vanilla brigadier parsing, permission levels and messages. The host is always the owner (level 4), with or without an `ops.json` entry, like a singleplayer world with cheats on. A command the player's level doesn't reach answers `Unknown or incomplete command`, exactly as an unknown one does. Parse errors are red, followed by a grey context line with the last 10 characters before the error, the rest red and underlined, and a red `<--[HERE]`. A command that parses but fails, such as `/difficulty` set to the current difficulty, answers with one red line. Players are named by name, `@s`, `@p`, `@a` or `@r`; `@e`, `@n` and selector options like `[limit=1]` are refused.
  - `/help [<command>]` (level 0) lists the commands the player can use, with vanilla usages such as `/gamerule <rule> [<value>]`.
  - `/seed` (level 0) shows the seed as a green `[number]`.
  - `/op <targets>` and `/deop <targets>` (level 3) add or remove `ops.json` entries in the world folder (written at once, vanilla format, level 4). `/deop` on the host removes its entry, but the host stays the owner.
  - `/difficulty [peaceful|easy|normal|hard]` (level 2) shows or sets the difficulty.
  - `/gamerule <rule> [<value>]` (level 2) shows or sets one of the 58 game rules, by its 26.3 id with or without `minecraft:` (`keep_inventory`, not `keepInventory`). Integers are checked against the rule's bounds.
  - `/time set <time>|day|noon|night|midnight`, `/time add <time>`, `/time pause`, `/time resume`, `/time query time` and `/time query gametime` (level 2) use the 26.3 clock syntax. A time takes `d` (24000 ticks), `s` (20) or `t`. A time marker moves the clock forward to the next one, so the day count keeps going. The game time (`/time query gametime` and `Time` in `level.dat`) keeps counting while the clock is paused or `advance_time` is off, and `/time set` and `/time add` don't change it. `/time` acts on the default clock of the command's dimension, as in 26.3: `minecraft:overworld` in the overworld, `minecraft:the_end` in the End (a clock of its own with no time markers), and none in the Nether, where every `/time` but `/time query gametime` answers `There is no default clock in dimension minecraft:the_nether`. `/execute in` picks the dimension.
  - `/weather clear|rain|thunder [<duration>]` (level 2) sets the server's weather. Without a duration it lasts vanilla's random time: 12000-180000 ticks clear, 12000-24000 rain and 3600-15600 thunder.
  - `/kill [<targets>]` (level 2) kills players; without a target it kills the one who typed it.
  - `/give <targets> <item> [<count>]` (level 2) gives items by id, with or without `minecraft:`, built like the kit's stacks. An item takes vanilla's `enchantments` or `stored_enchantments` component, as in `diamond_sword[enchantments={sharpness:5,"minecraft:looting":3}]` (levels 1 to 255), or the kit's shorthand `diamond_pickaxe+silk_touch+efficiency=5`. Items fill the selected hotbar slot, the offhand and the inventory like a pickup, with the pickup pop for each full stack that fits; what doesn't fit drops at the feet and can be picked up at once. An unknown item (`Unknown item 'minecraft:x'`), an unknown enchantment or any other component is refused with the vanilla error, and more than 100 stacks (`Can't give more than 6400 of [Stone]`) is refused before anything is given.
  - `/tp` and `/teleport` (level 2) take `<location>`, `<destination>`, `<targets> <location>` or `<targets> <destination>`. Locations are vanilla coordinates: absolute (whole x and z go to the block centre), `~` relative to the feet, or `^` local to the view. The message gives the position with six decimals like vanilla. A teleport stops the player, resets the fall height, and holds the player in place until the chunk at the destination has collision, so a `/tp` into land that isn't generated yet doesn't fall through it. Dead players aren't moved. Positions beyond 30 million blocks out or 20 million up or down are refused.
  - `/effect give <targets> <effect> [<seconds>|infinite] [<amplifier>] [<hideParticles>]` and `/effect clear [<targets>] [<effect>]` (level 2) know every 26.3 effect id from the language file. Without seconds an effect lasts 30 s; instant effects count their seconds as ticks and default to one. Regeneration, poison, hunger, absorption, resistance, fire resistance, nausea, slowness and weakness are applied, `infinite` included; instant health heals 2 hearts, doubled for each amplifier level, instant damage hurts like a harming potion and saturation feeds like vanilla. Any other effect answers `Unable to apply this effect`, as vanilla does when a stronger effect is already on. `hideParticles` is accepted and ignored, since no effect particles are drawn.
  - `/summon <entity> [<pos>] [<nbt>]` (level 2) summons one of the simulated kinds (see [World simulation](#world-simulation-integrated-server-thread)) at the feet or at the given position, with the vanilla NBT loaded over its fresh tag, e.g. `{NoAI:1b}`. Any other entity answers `Unable to summon entity`. The server thread answers, and `Summoned new Cow` (or the red failure, such as monsters in Peaceful) shows when it does. Command summons never touch the spawn eggs waiting for their answer.
  - `/gamemode <gamemode> [<target>]` (level 2) sets `survival`, `creative`, `adventure` or `spectator` for the player who typed it or for the targets. The player who typed it sees `Set own game mode to Creative Mode`; for another player the typer sees `Set <name>'s game mode to ...` and the target gets `Your game mode has been updated to ...` (only while `send_command_feedback` is on). A player already in that mode is left alone without a message. The change is written to the player file at once.
  - `/clear [<targets>] [<item>] [<maxCount>]` (level 2) removes matching items (`*`, an id or a `#tag`) from the inventory, armour, offhand, crafting grid and cursor; `maxCount` 0 only counts them.
  - `/experience` and `/xp` (level 2) `add`, `set` and `query` points or levels with vanilla's float maths and the level-up chime.
  - `/enchant <targets> <enchantment> [<level>]` (level 2) enchants the held item with vanilla's checks (no item, incompatible, level above the maximum).
  - `/setblock <pos> <block> [destroy|keep|replace|strict]` and `/fill <from> <to> <block> [outline|hollow|destroy|strict|keep|replace [<filter>]]` (level 2) take vanilla block states (`id[name=value]`), block predicates with `#tags` for the filter, and the `max_block_modifications` limit. The edits run on the level's server thread, which answers when it is done. A position that is not loaded answers `That position is not loaded`.
  - `/spawnpoint [<targets>] [<pos>] [<rotation>]` (level 2) sets each player's forced respawn point in the command's dimension, with vanilla's message.
  - `/locate structure|biome|poi <id|#tag>` (level 2) searches on the level's thread like vanilla's `findNearestMapStructure` and `findClosestBiome3d`, with a time cap; a closed level opens for the search.
  - `/execute in <dimension> ...`, `/execute positioned <pos> ...` and `/execute run <command>` (level 2). `in` knows all three dimensions, open or not, and scales x and z by the dimension's coordinate scale; a `/tp` after it moves the player into that dimension. The other subcommands (`as`, `at`, `if`, `store` and the rest) are not parsed.
  - `/kit [<targets>]` (level 4, a fork command) gives `IW4L_MINECRAFT_KIT` again, re-read from the `.env` each time, through `/give`.
  - Success messages follow the `send_command_feedback` rule, and other operators see them grey and in italics when `log_admin_commands` is on.
- Suggestions like vanilla's `CommandSuggestions`: a command line shows the list of completions above the input (Tab writes the selected one, Up and Down move, Shift+Tab goes back), with the rest of the selected completion in grey after the cursor, the grey usage of the next argument or the parse error above the line, and the input coloured per argument. A chat line without `/` completes player names on Tab. Ids, tags, players, game rules, coordinates (the block in view), dimensions and the fork's `+enchantment` kit syntax are suggested.

## Dimensions

All three dimensions run on one `Server` with a `Level` each (see [Crate layout](#crate-layout)). Every level reads its rules from its 26.3 dimension type and saves into its own `dimensions/minecraft/<name>/` folder.

### Levels and the switch (round 7, track SWITCH)
- **Opening:** the overworld opens with the world, as in vanilla, where a new world's spawn is found. The Nether and the End open when a player needs them: `LevelLoader` builds the level on a `minecraft-level-load` thread and runs the arrival search (portal search or platform) there. A generator that panics fails that level, never the game: the player is sent back with a red line (`minecraft:the_end could not be loaded: ...`), and the next request tries again.
- **Closing:** a level nobody is in saves and closes after 300 ticks (15 s, vanilla's empty time), on a `minecraft-level-save` thread that the next opening of that dimension waits for. A dead player keeps their respawn level open, and a `temp` world keeps its empty levels open. A player who comes back within the 15 s gets the open level shown again (`TerrainStream::reshow`).
- **What is shared:** the world spawn, the game rules, the difficulty, the weather and both clocks belong to the server, as in 26.3. The overworld clock drives day and night, the End has a clock of its own and the Nether none; the weather keeps running while nobody is in the overworld. Only the level the local player is in ticks.
- **The switch:** a player keeps everything (inventory, health, UUID, respawn point) and only changes level. Leaving closes the container screen (loose crafting items go back or drop in the old level), hands over stacks the old level already let them pick up (dropped there when they don't fit), cancels a waiting item use and pearl landings, and stops eating and blocking. Until the arrival's chunks have collision and a section is drawn the player is held still and invulnerable (vanilla's `isChangingDimension`) and the new level only streams, so no mob can hit someone who can't move yet. A trip waits while the player flies a remote killstreak.
- **The loading screen** is vanilla's `LevelLoadingScreen` with "Loading terrain...": the nether portal sprite for a trip from or to the Nether, the end portal starfield for the End, and the title panorama under the menu background otherwise (the downloaded pack only has a placeholder panorama, so it shows a flat colour). It stays up 0.5 s after the player is let in.
- **Respawn:** a dead player respawns in the dimension of their respawn point (bed, anchor or `/spawnpoint`), else at the world spawn in the overworld. The respawn level opens while the player is still dead, so it is ready when MW2 shows them alive again; a respawn into another dimension is a switch with the panorama screen. A missing, empty or obstructed bed or anchor is cleared with vanilla's message.
- **Ways between dimensions:** nether portals, the end portal and the End's exit portal (below), death, `/execute in <dimension> run tp ...`, and `IW4L_MINECRAFT_DIMENSION` at start (see [Testing workflow](#testing-workflow)).
- **MW2 in other dimensions:** guns, grenades and craters work in every dimension with vanilla blast resistance. Air support (predator, airstrikes, harrier, helicopters, AC-130 and the care package markers) is refused under a ceiling (the Nether): the streak stays on the hotbar and the chat says why. Projectile and script-model blasts from before a switch leave the new level alone.

### The Nether (round 7, track NETHER)
- **Dimension rules** come from each dimension type of the 26.3 data pack (`minecraft_survival/src/dimension.rs`): coordinate scale, heights, ceiling, cardinal light, the bed and straw bed rules, whether respawn anchors work, whether water evaporates, fast lava and portal piglins.
- **The Nether level:** its sea level is 32 and its lava flows faster (one level a block, every 10 ticks, so 7 blocks out). Its terrain, fortresses, bastions, ruined portals and fossils generate, and the bastion's piglins are kept. Blocks, fluids, mobs and dropped items are lit from above and below alike, as vanilla's Nether cardinal light. Explosions can start fires (`ServerExplosion.createFire`).
- **Nether portals** (`portal.rs`, `portal_forcer.rs`, adapter `render_anim/src/minecraft_portal.rs`):
  - Standing in a portal sends a survival player after 80 ticks (4 s) and a creative one at once, from the two game rules; outside it the timer falls by 4 a tick, and a 10-tick cooldown (saved as `PortalCooldown`) keeps the arrival portal from sending you back. End portals go through the same timer and cooldown with no wait. `allow_entering_nether_using_portals` is honoured.
  - x and z scale by 8 (into the Nether ÷8) inside the world border. The exit is the nearest portal block within 16 blocks in the Nether or 128 in the overworld, read from loaded and saved chunks and never generated, the closest by distance and then the lowest; else a new portal is built like vanilla's `PortalForcer.createPortal`, on the nearest floor with room or on its own obsidian platform. The player keeps their place in the portal, turns with its axis, and is moved out of walls.
  - The purple swirl fades in over 4 s with the trigger sound, container screens close, the portal loading screen shows behind the trip, and the travel sound plays on arrival.
  - Overworld portals spawn zombified piglins on hard (`NetherPortalBlock.randomTick`).
- **Nether mobs:** zombified piglins (neutral, their group turns on whoever hits one, golden swords), magma cubes (armoured, fire-proof, split), wither skeletons (stone swords, the wither effect and black hearts), ghasts (they float, warn and fire fireballs of power 1; an MW2 bullet or a left-click swing sends a fireball back along the aim and it kills its ghast; a grenade only pushes it) and blazes (slow fall, climbing, three-shot small fireball volleys that set you alight). The fire-immune types shrug off fire and lava. Fortresses spawn their own monsters on nether bricks, structure spawn overrides (swamp huts, monuments, outposts) apply, and monster spawners spawn their mob.
- **Rules:**
  - Water evaporates: a water bucket only hisses, mined ice leaves nothing, a placed wet sponge dries.
  - Beds explode in the Nether and the End with power 5 and fire, through the vanilla explosion damage; a death to one reads `<name> was killed by [Intentional Game Design]`. Straw beds only break there.
  - In the overworld a bed in reach sets the respawn point ("Respawn point set") and says when you can't sleep, is too far away, obstructed or occupied; sleeping itself is not done.
  - Respawn anchors charge with glowstone (up to 4, from either hand), set the respawn point in the Nether, and explode with power 5 and fire anywhere else; flowing water beside it or any water on top keeps the blocks around it.
  - A dead player respawns beside their anchor (spending a charge, facing it, with its deplete sound) or bed, in whatever dimension it is; a missing, empty or obstructed one is cleared with "You have no home bed or charged respawn anchor, or it was obstructed", and the player goes to the overworld spawn. The End's exit portal takes a player to the same spot without spending a charge or playing the sound, as vanilla's `PlayerList.respawn` with `keepAllPlayerData`.
  - Compasses (to the world spawn), recovery compasses (to the last death) and clocks (the sun) turn with a wobbling needle in the hotbar, the inventory and the first-person hand, and spin where their target is in another dimension or the dimension has no daytime.
  - Overlay messages (the action bar above the hotbar) show, as vanilla's `Gui.setOverlayMessage`.
- **Sky:** the Nether has no sky, sun, moon, stars or clouds, only its fog colour, as vanilla.
- What is left (N6 and smaller gaps) is under [Limitations](#dimensions-1).

### The End (round 7, track END)
- **Getting there** (`minecraft_survival/src/the_end.rs`, adapter `render_anim/src/minecraft_end.rs`):
  - Eyes of ender fill end portal frames (with the fill sound) and open the portal once all 12 frames of an inward ring hold one (with the portal sound). Thrown anywhere else, an eye flies about 12 blocks toward the nearest stronghold, rising 8, and after 4 s drops (4 in 5) or shatters.
  - End portals and gateways are drawn with vanilla's starfield layers. Stepping into an end portal goes to the End at once with the portal travel sound, onto the obsidian platform at 100, 49, 0 facing west, keeping the pitch; the platform is rebuilt on every arrival and drops what it breaks.
  - The End sky is its tiled texture with no sun, moon, stars or clouds. The arena around the island centre stays loaded while a player is within 192 blocks.
- **The dragon fight** (`minecraft_survival/src/the_end/dragon_fight.rs`, saved per End level in `dimensions/minecraft/the_end/data/minecraft/ender_dragon_fight.dat` with vanilla's keys):
  - The first visit builds the inactive exit podium and spawns the dragon, which flies its holding pattern between the 24 path nodes, beats its wings and growls.
  - It fights back with all eleven vanilla phases (`minecraft_terrain/src/ender_dragon.rs`), picking the players of the End level, never a fixed one. At the end of a circling path it may land (one in crystals + 3) or swoop at the attackable player nearest the podium and spit a fireball (`dragon_fireball.rs`) that bursts into a dragon's breath cloud: Harming II, growing from 3 to 7 blocks over 30 s. Breaking a crystal makes it swoop at the player who broke it.
  - Landing, it perches on top of the exit portal's pillar, turns to a player within 20 blocks, roars for 2 s and breathes a Harming I flame cloud on the podium, four times, then takes off; with nobody near it takes off or charges a player it sees within 150 blocks. More than 50 damage while it sits makes it take off at once.
  - Its wings fling players and mobs they sweep (out from its body by 4 divided by their distance from it, in blocks a tick, through MW2's `push_velocity` for players) and hurt them for 5 while it flies; its head and neck bite for 10. It breaks the blocks its head, neck and body fly through, without drops, except `#dragon_immune` ones (end stone, obsidian, iron bars, bedrock...), which slow it down, and nothing with `mob_griefing` off.
  - Glass bottles in reach of its breath (within 2 blocks) fill with dragon's breath and shrink the cloud by half a block.
  - End crystals sit on the pillars over fire, explode with power 6 when hit (not when a blast destroys them) and can be placed on obsidian or bedrock. The dragon heals 1 every 10 ticks from the nearest crystal within reach, shown by a beam; breaking that crystal costs it 10 on the head.
  - A pink boss bar shows its health to the players within 192 blocks, and its world fog hides the End sky.
  - MW2 bullets, grenades and Minecraft hits hurt it through its 8 body parts with one flat MW2 hit location: the head and neck take the damage, the other parts a quarter and 1; only players and explosions hurt it.
  - Its death is vanilla's: it flies to the podium, rises for 10 s under white rays with a level-wide roar, and drops 12000 experience on the first kill (level 68 from 0) and 500 after. The exit portal then opens, the dragon egg appears on the pillar after the first kill, and the next of the 20 end gateways appears 96 blocks out at height 75.
  - The dragon egg jumps up to 15 blocks away (7 up or down) onto solid ground with a stream of portal particles when it is right-clicked, hit by hand or shot (`minecraft_terrain/src/dragon_egg.rs`); a creative player's instant break still takes it.
  - Four end crystals on the exit portal's rim respawn it: the portal goes out, the crystals beam at each pillar in turn, which explodes and is rebuilt with an invulnerable crystal, and a new dragon comes (500 experience, no egg). Breaking one of the four stops it.
- **Going home:** the exit portal takes players to their respawn point (bed, anchor or `/spawnpoint`, else the world spawn) keeping everything. The first time, the credits roll from the jar's texts (the End Poem with the player's name, the credits, the post-credits line) over the End starfield while the trip loads behind them; Space speeds them up, Ctrl more, Up scrolls back and Escape skips them. `seenCredits` is saved per player, so they show once.
- **Elytra and rockets:** an elytra in the chest slot glides on MW2 movement (a fresh jump in the air), wears 1 durability a second, hurts on wall hits, and firework rockets boost it.
- **Shulkers** (`minecraftoss-entities` `shulker.rs`, `shulker_bullet.rs`, `world/shulkers.rs`, `world/shulker_bullets.rs`, drawn by `minecraft_terrain/src/shulker_render.rs`):
  - They cling to the face of a sturdy block (floor, wall or ceiling), peek now and then with the open and close sounds, and turn their head in the plane of their face. Without a face to cling to, with water in their cell or a block over the lid they move to another face or teleport up to 8 blocks with portal particles. They are drawn with their colour (`Color`), lid and head, and keep face, peek, colour and shell through a save.
  - Closed, the shell gives 20 armour (from its first close on, as vanilla) and turns arrows and tridents away. Below half health one hit in four teleports them. They drop a shulker shell half the time and 5 experience, never despawn by distance and stay in peaceful.
  - With a player within 16 blocks in sight they open fully and fire a bullet every 1 to 5.5 seconds. Bullets step along the axes toward the target, home in, glow, spin and leave an end rod trail; with no target they fall. A hit does 2 hearts (scaled by difficulty, "was slain by Shulker") and gives levitation for 10 s when it lands (not through a full shield block or in creative). A struck mob takes 4 and floats; a struck open shulker teleports and may leave a copy of its colour. MW2 guns, blasts and punches pop bullets with crit sparks; a bullet hitting a block puffs. Peaceful removes them. Bullets save their course, owner and target.
  - **Levitation** lifts players at vanilla's rate (`(0.05 Ã— (level) âˆ’ vy) Ã— 0.2` a tick with the 0.98 drag: about 0.9 blocks a second at level 1, 9 blocks in 10 s) with MW2 air control, on climbables too, but not in water, lava or creative flight, and it stops elytra flight. **Slow falling** (potions) caps the fall at about 10 blocks a second and slows a glide's sinking. While either is on, the fall distance restarts, so landing after them only counts the fall from where they ended. Mobs float and fall slowly the same way. Milk and the totem clear both, and `/effect` gives them.

## Crate layout

Since the crate split ([CRATE-SPLIT.md](CRATE-SPLIT.md)), the Minecraft game state lives outside the render crate. New game state goes into `minecraft_survival`, kept per dimension in a `Level` or per player (by UUID) in a `Player`, never in the adapter and never as a single global player.

- **`crates/minecraft_survival`** (edition 2024, no bevy): the headless game state and rules.
  - `server.rs`: one `Server` per open world:
    - `globals`: seed, registries, the world folder, game rules, difficulty, the world spawn with its dimension, the overworld and End clocks, the weather, the chat `CommandServer`, game data, the autosave timer, the saves of closing levels and the `/locate` searches waiting for a level.
    - `levels`: a `Level` per open `Dimension`. `opening`: a `LevelLoader` per dimension being built. `players`: a `Player` per `PlayerUuid`.
    - Joining (`join`, the kit and the saved player file), opening and closing levels, respawn preparation, the autosave and the closing save run from here.
  - `level.rs`: a `Level` holds its `dimension`, `entities` (the level's server thread and the client copies of its mobs), `containers`, `terrain` (the `TerrainStream`, block scene, environment and dimension rules), the container `openers` per player, `empty_ticks` and, in the End, the `dragon_fight`. Block writes, container ticks, chunk streaming and leaving a level run on it.
  - `player.rs`: a `Player` holds the UUID, name, dimension, place, survival stats, game mode, death watch, inventory, selected slot, own ender chest, respawn point, last death, portal cooldown and `seenCredits`, the hand (swing, item cooldowns, blocking, trident charge, mining and placing timers), steps and falls, elytra, portal and End travel state, and the dimension switch in progress (`switching`) or held for a remote killstreak (`held`). `travel_to` and `teleport_into` move a living player to another dimension; `switch_to` is the immediate switch the respawn uses.
  - Dimensions: `switch.rs` (the loading reason, `determineLevelLoadingReason`), `respawn.rs` (where a player respawns), `dimension.rs` (a dimension's rules), `portal.rs` and `portal_forcer.rs` (the portal timer, the destination and the portal search and build), `respawn_block.rs` (beds and anchors), `the_end.rs` (end portals, the platform, the exit portal and the credits) and `the_end/dragon_fight.rs` (the per-level fight, saved as `ender_dragon_fight.dat`).
  - Rules: `survival.rs` (health, hunger, air, fire, effects, XP, falls), `death.rs`, `offhand.rs` (shield, totem, hand use), `guns.rs`, `gear.rs` (what the MW2 body wears and holds), `trident.rs`, `impulse.rs` (wind charge and Riptide launches), `elytra.rs`, `cooldowns.rs`, `creative.rs` (creative slot setting), `commands.rs` (the `CommandWorld` host, per named player) and `command_blocks.rs` (block access for `/setblock` and `/fill` on the server thread).
  - Storage and data: `save.rs` (world folder, `level.dat`, the `data/minecraft` files, player files), `player_nbt.rs` (what a player file holds, in vanilla's keys), `entities.rs` (the server thread handle and the client copies of its mobs, items and orbs) and `data.rs` (recipes, loot, stations and enchantments, read from the jar once per world).
- **`crates/minecraft_net`** (serde only): the shared ids `Dimension` and `PlayerUuid`. The network messages come with multiplayer.
- **`crates/minecraft_terrain`** (edition 2021, so no let-chains): world generation, the `TerrainStream`, the level server thread (`server.rs`: mobs, items, projectiles, the dragon, block updates, hoppers), containers, crafting, stations, game modes, item-use rules, packs, particles and all meshing. It stayed whole in the split, because its scene, stream and server types depend on each other.
- **`crates/minecraft_commands`**: the command tree, `CommandServer`, the `CommandWorld` trait, the suggestion engine and the chat protocol.
- **`crates/render_anim`**: the Bevy adapter.

## Multiplayer today

Everything here comes from reading the code. No multiplayer session was run.

### MW2 netcode
The MW2 netcode is host-authoritative and goes through the `iw4l-master` QUIC relay. It needs a self-hosted master; there is no direct IP connect and no LAN discovery. Player movement, MW2 models, gun switching and PvP are synced.

### The Minecraft world is single player
- It runs only on the host. `render_anim/src/minecraft_world.rs` returns early without a local `AuthorityWorld`, so a joining client most likely never finishes loading the world.
- Nothing Minecraft goes over the network: blocks, mobs, items, inventories, containers, hunger, time of day and weather are all host-only.
- What is ready for friends: the state is per player and per level (`Player` per UUID, `Level` per dimension), player files are per UUID, container openers, thrown items, pearls, potion hits, the dragon's targets and the End credits are per player, `ops.json` knows players by the vanilla offline UUID of their name (a friend has level 0 until `/op`), commands act on the player they name, and the chat and suggestion types (`Serverbound`, `Clientbound` with translatable text, `CommandHints`) are serde.
- What still assumes one player: the `Entities` handle talks to the level's server thread as player `0`, and what the thread hands back for a player (picked-up stacks, XP, item uses, egg and summon answers) has no player key yet. Chunks stream around the host only, only the host's level ticks, and one MW2 collision world (`sim::voxel`) mirrors the host's level, so bots and MW2 entities follow the host into other dimensions. Death drops are spawned for the host only, and death messages go only to the host's chat.

## Saving

- **Where and when:** `iw4l-artifacts\saves\<name>` next to the exe, written every 5 minutes (6000 ticks) on background threads, when a match ends and when the game quits through the menu. A level is also saved when it closes (15 s after the last player leaves it), and a player file right after a game mode change.
  - `IW4L_MINECRAFT_WORLD` names the world (default `world`; letters, digits, `_` and `-` only). `temp` plays a throw-away world: its chunks live in a session folder that is deleted afterwards. A world that another copy of the game holds falls back to a temporary world with a warning.
  - `IW4L_MINECRAFT_SEED` only seeds a new world; an existing world keeps its seed and the log warns that the variable is ignored.
  - `IW4L_MINECRAFT_WEATHER` sets the weather once at the start, only when it is set. `IW4L_MINECRAFT_KIT` only goes to a player whose file has no `Inventory` yet (otherwise the log says `Minecraft kit skipped`); in `temp` it is given on every start. `/kit` gives it again in game.
- **File layout** (vanilla 26.3, so a world folder opens in Minecraft 26.3 and a world from Minecraft 26.3 opens here). 26.3 no longer uses `DIM-1` and `DIM1`.
  - **Per world, at the root:**
    - `level.dat`: the world spawn with its dimension, game time, difficulty (`difficulty_settings`), `allowCommands=1`, the world's default `GameType` (read and kept, 0 for a new world, not changed by `/gamemode`), `singleplayer_uuid` (the host's UUID as four ints, whose player file vanilla loads for the singleplayer owner), the version and `DataVersion` 5023. Worlds from older builds, which wrote `allowCommands=0`, get 1 on their next save.
    - `session.lock`, and `ops.json` in the vanilla format (`uuid`, `name`, `level`, `bypassesPlayerLimit`).
    - `data/minecraft/`: `world_gen_settings.dat` (the seed), `world_clocks.dat` (the overworld and End clocks, each with its `/time pause` flag), `game_rules.dat` and `weather.dat` (`clear_weather_time`, `rain_time`, `thunder_time`, `raining`, `thundering`; the world opens already raining when it rained).
  - **Per dimension**, in `dimensions/minecraft/<overworld|the_nether|the_end>/`:
    - `region/r.<x>.<z>.mca`: blocks and block entities.
    - `entities/`: mobs (kinds the game doesn't simulate yet are kept as they were saved), villager gossip, dropped items with their components as NBT, XP orbs, tridents with their thrower, end crystals and the dragon.
    - `poi/` and `data/`. The End's `data/minecraft/ender_dragon_fight.dat` holds the dragon fight with vanilla's keys: whether the dragon was killed before, its UUID, the exit portal, the gateways still to come and the respawn in progress.
  - **Per player:** `players/data/<uuid>.dat`, named by the vanilla offline UUID of the MW2 player name (`OfflinePlayer:<name>`, UUID v3). Renaming the MW2 player starts a new file, as on a vanilla offline server.
  - `level.dat`, the `data/minecraft` files and the player files keep a `_old` copy that is read when the file is missing or broken. A file that stays unreadable gives a warning, and that part starts again.
  - **Per game install, not per world** (as vanilla keeps them in the game folder): `iw4l-artifacts\settings.cfg` (keys and options), `command_history.txt` (the last 50 commands) and `hotbar.nbt` (the creative saved hotbars).
- **Game rules** start at the vanilla defaults. `max_minecart_speed` is left out, because it needs the experimental minecart flag. Rules from other versions or mods in `game_rules.dat` are kept as they are, and values outside a rule's bounds are clamped. `/gamerule` changes them in game. Rules with an effect: `keep_inventory`, `show_death_messages`, `send_command_feedback`, `log_admin_commands`, `advance_time`, `advance_weather`, `spawn_mobs`, `mob_griefing`, `mob_drops`, `block_drops` (also for blocks the player mines or shoots, and their XP), `entity_drops`, `random_tick_speed`, `fire_spread_radius_around_player`, `max_snow_accumulation_height`, `water_source_conversion`, `lava_source_conversion`, `tnt_explodes`, `tnt_explosion_drop_decay`, `block_explosion_drop_decay`, `spawner_blocks_work`, `fall_damage`, `fire_damage`, `drowning_damage`, `natural_health_regeneration`, `max_block_modifications`, `allow_entering_nether_using_portals`, `players_nether_portal_default_delay` and `players_nether_portal_creative_delay`. The others are stored and saved only. A pre-26 `GameRules` compound in an old `level.dat` is not carried over.
- **The player file** uses vanilla's keys and tag types (checked with javap):
  - Game mode: `playerGameType`, `previousPlayerGameType` (-1 for none) and the `abilities` compound. On load the abilities follow the saved mode, and a creative player saved in the air is still flying.
  - Items: `Inventory` (the 36 storage slots, each with a `Slot` byte, `id`, `count` and `components`), `equipment` (`head`, `chest`, `legs`, `feet`, `offhand`, `body`, `saddle`), `SelectedItemSlot`, and the player's own ender chest as `EnderItems`. Stacks on the cursor or in a crafting grid are saved into the inventory, as vanilla's menu puts them back.
  - Survival: `Health`, `AbsorptionAmount`, `foodLevel`, `foodTickTimer`, `foodSaturationLevel`, `foodExhaustionLevel`, `XpLevel`, `XpP`, `XpTotal`, `Score`, `Air`, `Fire`, `active_effects` (an infinite effect as -1) and the effect modifiers in `attributes`. A player saved dead comes back dead and respawns.
  - Place: `Pos`, `Motion`, `Rotation`, `OnGround`, `fall_distance`, `Dimension` and `UUID`. The player comes back where they left, in that dimension, facing the same way; the loading screen waits until the chunks there have collision.
  - `respawn` (dimension, position, yaw, pitch and `forced`), `LastDeathLocation`, `PortalCooldown` and `seenCredits`.
  - The file is merged: keys the game does not know (`recipeBook`, `entered_nether_pos`, `warden_spawn_tracker`, effects it does not simulate, ...) stay as they are, and keys it owns are removed when they become empty, so armour taken off or an ended effect does not come back.
- **Block entities** are saved with their chunk and follow their blocks: a broken block's block entity leaves the file, and a placed block gets vanilla's default one.
  - Chests (also trapped and copper chests), barrels and shulker boxes keep `Items` and `CustomName`. A structure chest rolls its loot when first opened and is then saved with `Items` and without `LootTable`, so it never fills again.
  - Furnaces, smokers and blast furnaces keep their progress, fuel and `RecipesUsed`, so experience smelted before a restart is paid when the output is taken. Brewing stands keep their brew and fuel, campfires their food and cooking times.
  - Hoppers, dispensers, droppers and comparators are saved from the level's server thread, only when they change.
  - Like vanilla, all of these stop while their chunk is unloaded and go on when it loads again.
- **Checked by** the end-to-end test `crates/minecraft_survival/tests/vanilla_world.rs`. It fills a player, a chest and a cooking furnace and sets the weather in a world folder, saves, opens the world again and checks that everything comes back. It also checks that every key of the player file, `level.dat`, `weather.dat` and the block entities has vanilla 26.3's tag type, and that vanilla's block entity codecs load and save the chest and the furnace unchanged.
- **Not saved, and why:**
  - MW2 guns (`iw4:weapon/<n>`) in the inventory, a chest or on the ground: the number is the match's weapon index, and Minecraft has no such item. The guns you own come back to the hotbar on every spawn.
  - What the game does not simulate yet: recipe book unlocks, advancements and statistics (no `advancements/` or `stats/` folders), item cooldowns, the F5 view, sign text, lectern books, jukebox discs, decorated pot, chiseled bookshelf and shelf items, bees in hives, brushable block loot, crafter items, beacons, conduits, bells, spawners, trial spawners and vaults, sculk blocks, command blocks and enchanting tables. Their block entities stay as generated or as Minecraft saved them. A name or components on a placed block item are dropped, except on shulker boxes.
  - Thrown snowballs, eggs, pearls, potions, effect clouds and fireballs: they live a few seconds. Tridents are saved.
  - A trident waiting in a chunk that was never loaded is lost when the world closes, because the entity storage can't yet merge into a chunk it hasn't loaded. Vanilla queues that chunk and saves it.
  - A hopper feeding the player's chest or furnace, or a comparator reading one, works on the level's own copy of it, which the player does not see. That waits for one container truth on the server ("Finish survival").
  - A match that ends goes through MW2's intermission, which resets health, hunger and XP before the save. Quitting through the menu keeps them.

## Limitations

One list of what is open after round 7. Multiplayer is the next roadmap step; N6 and X6 are left for "Finish survival".

### Multiplayer
- No Minecraft multiplayer yet (see [Multiplayer today](#multiplayer-today)). The chat is local, and death messages go only to the host's chat, already resolved in the host's language; vanilla broadcasts them as translatable messages.
- **Friends in different dimensions** need what the single player doesn't: every occupied level ticking (only the host's level ticks today), a server tick with several players (the level thread still talks to player `0`), chunk streaming around each player with a shared thread budget, and an MW2 collision world per dimension. Today one collision world (`sim::voxel`) mirrors the host's level, so bots and every MW2 entity follow the host into the Nether or the End.

### Dimensions
- **N6, the rest of the Nether:** piglins (the bastion's wait dormant), piglin brutes, hoglins and zoglins, striders (they never spawn: their spawn rules are not ported), piglin bartering, lodestones and lodestone compasses. Beds only set the respawn point; sleeping through the night is not done.
- **Nether details:** no biome particles (ash, spores) or biome ambience, no smoke of fireballs and blazes, no spawner and magma cube flames, no respawn anchor particles or hum, and compasses that are dropped or held in third person don't turn. The MW2 player and weapon models keep the overworld's two fixed entity lights in the Nether.
- **X6, the outer End:** end gateways don't throw players or pearls to the outer islands (no return gateway either) and have no beam, item frames don't show or drop their item (the elytra in end ships), and the End sky has no flashes.
- **Shulkers:** they are not solid to the MW2 player, and an opening lid pushes mobs but not players. Looting doesn't raise the shell chance (no weapon reaches the loot context), skeleton arrows and blasts don't pop bullets, and there are no effect icons, so levitation and slow falling show only by their behaviour.
- **The dragon fight:** players' hits don't knock the dragon back, and a sitting dragon's immunity to arrows is missing (no arrow hits it yet). Not drawn: the breath particles from its mouth while it lands and flames, the fireball's breath trail, the explosion particle where it breaks blocks, and its dissolve and explosion particles while it dies. A crystal's blast reaches mobs, players and other crystals one tick late, and a player killed by one is "blown up" without the name of who set it off. A respawn in progress is saved with its stage's start time. There is no boss music.
- **Other End gaps:** the credits have no vignette and no music (the game plays no Minecraft music), the elytra and its glide pose are not drawn on the MW2 body (it plays its in-air animation), the eye of ender's frame smoke puffs and the push up when a frame grows are missing, and chorus fruit doesn't teleport.
- **The switch:** a level-wide script blast and killstreak turret bullets fired before a switch still reach the new level, and MW2 player damage from old ordnance is not filtered, as the MW2 map is shared. A respawn in the same dimension shows no loading screen (the player only waits for their chunk's collision, so terrain may still fade in), and respawns are not spread at random over vanilla's radius of 10.

### Commands and screens
- Commands: no `@e`, `@n` or selector options, no mob targets, no `/tp` rotation or `facing` arguments, no item components other than enchantments, no effects beyond the eleven simulated ones and the instant three, no summoning of entities the server doesn't simulate, no `/time rate`, timelines or `/time of`, no `/defaultgamemode`, only `in`, `positioned` and `run` for `/execute`, and no functions or command blocks.
- Suggestions are keyboard only (vanilla also picks a line with the mouse), and `/op` and `/deop` suggest every online player.
- Creative inventory: tabs and the scroller take the mouse only, not the pad; the middle button picks a stack in the creative screen only; C (save hotbar) is also MW2's crouch.
- Chat gaps: no clickable or hoverable text, underline or per-word italics, no chat settings (opacity, width, scale, line spacing), no mouse selection, no message signing or narration. The input line scrolls to keep the cursor in view without vanilla's remembered scroll position.
- GUI gaps: no 3D enchanting book, smithing armour stand, recipe book, effects panel or rarity colours. Characters missing from the font show as gaps.

### Rendering
- Chest, ender chest, copper chest and shulker box lids open and close like vanilla, but an opening shulker box does not grow its collision or push anyone. Other block entities never animate: no bell swing, banner wave or enchanting table book. There are no banner or shield patterns and no sign text. Heads use the default skin, and the conduit always shows its inactive shell.
- Not drawn: the copper golem statue, and the falling rain and snow streaks (rain darkens the sky, splashes on the ground and sounds, but nothing falls through the air). Each half of a double chest is lit separately.
- Other blocks' display ticks (drips, leaves, torches, candles, biome particles) do nothing yet. The animation clock keeps running while paused.
- Lava fog and underwater fog follow the eyes, not a third-person camera.
- Third person: the arms keep MW2's gun grip, so vanilla's arm swing and use poses are missing, and armour trims, heads and carved pumpkins on the head, the elytra and burning flames are not drawn on the body. A corpse wears no gear. The camera stops on block collision shapes, so glass, fences and snow layers stop it where vanilla's visual shapes would let it through or closer, and MW2 scope overlays are not checked in third person yet.
- Glint: mob armour, items held by mobs, items on campfires and thrown bottles o' enchanting have none, because they reach the renderer as bare item ids. The glint streaks in the world are sized for a 512-pixel atlas, so their density can differ a little from vanilla. A block item gets one flat glint over its icon instead of one per face, and the glint speed and strength are fixed at the vanilla defaults (0.5 and 0.75).
- An ignored render error loses that frame, so an error that repeats every frame would flicker the screen. bevy also prints `Caught rendering error` to the console, not to the game log.
- A model whose index stream has not been uploaded yet casts no sun shadow for that one frame, and an effect mesh past its 16384-vertex or 24576-index buffer is not drawn that frame.

### Movement
- The bush and cobweb slowdown has no momentum carry, and the Weaving effect is ignored.
- Shallow-lava details differ from vanilla. Powder snow neither slows nor freezes.
- No ground jump at a ladder, no climbing in fluids, and an open trapdoor above a ladder doesn't count as part of it.
- Sneak edge: no back-off while in the air, and it checks blocks only, not entities.
- Flight: the MW2 body plays its in-air animation, there is no flying FOV change, and a fast touchdown can play the MW2 landing sound. Flying close over the ground uses air drag, where vanilla uses the ground's friction for the tick it leaves the ground.

### Survival and combat
- Hand mining counts as a diamond pickaxe with the held item's enchantments, so a bare hand or a wrong tool still drops items.
- Protection doesn't lessen drowning or the poison and wither effects, which hurt inside the vendored status tick, and blast protection's explosion knockback resistance is not applied.
- Death drops are spawned on the host for the one local player, by the per-frame update in `render_anim` with `minecraft_survival::death::death_drops`, not by the server thread. Anvil, enchanting and other bench inputs go back to the inventory when the screen closes and then drop with it. Items that fall into lava or fire burn like any dropped item. A match end still resets the XP level to 0.
- Shield: axes don't disable it, and MW2 bullets ignore it. No simulated mob carries an axe (vindicators and piglin brutes are missing), so the disabling only matters once players fight each other.
- The totem plays no animation.
- Riptide has no one-second spin attack and no spin pose. The MW2 body keeps its normal hold while charging a trident. A trident or wind charge that kills the player gives the generic death message.
- Game modes: spectators have no spectator menu, can't look through a mob's eyes, see block insides instead of vanilla's x-ray view and keep the MW2 crosshair. MW2 bullets still stop at a spectator's hitbox (without damage), the third-person body still holds the last gun, mobs still spawn around a spectator, and fast flight can outrun the chunk streaming, so the terrain fills in late. In creative, buckets and bottles still empty and fill like in survival (vanilla keeps the full bucket). Adventure ignores the `can_break` item component, honours `can_place_on` only for plain blocks, and a water bottle can still turn dirt into mud. The MW2 bullet rules follow the local player's mode, whoever fired: while the host is in adventure no bullet mines, and while the host is in creative no bullet break drops, bots' bullets included. MW2 effects that scripts apply without damage, such as a flashbang's blinding, may still reach creative players, and MW2 bots still shoot at them (harmlessly).
- MW2 blast damage on mobs is MW2 damage x 0.8 (bullets x 0.2), not vanilla's explosion formula, so a frag kills a 20-health mob it can see anywhere in its reach. MW2 craters are full spheres (the claymore's cone limits only its damage) and appear a server tick after the MW2 explosion. Mobs can't set off claymores. Kills by bot explosives give no XP.
- TNT explosions don't hurt mobs or players; only creeper explosions do.
- MW2 perks and scripts that heal or resize a live player's health (Last Stand and Final Stand revives, the low-health overlay's max reset) do nothing on Minecraft maps; only Minecraft heals. Since MW2's maximum health stays at its spawn value, MW2's blood overlay and pain vision stay on while the hearts are low.

### Blocks and items
- Buckets and flint and steel wait for the server, with no client prediction.
- No Unbreaking on flint and steel, and lava doesn't set blocks on fire.
- Books can't be placed on a lectern.
- A shulker box or bundle that burns as an item loses its contents instead of spilling them. Experience orbs don't burn. A burning item keeps burning in a water cauldron.
- Trapped chests give no redstone signal.
- The client judges mushroom and crop light from its own light; where the server disagrees it refuses the block and drops it as an item. Falling stalactites hurt nobody.
- Redstone covers only levers, buttons and the powered sounds of doors, trapdoors and gates.
- Spawn eggs for unsimulated mobs (fox, rabbit, goat and others) spawn nothing, and the egg is kept. Clicking a spawner with an egg doesn't change its mob.
- The fire charge uses flint and steel's block-update flags.
- Tool transformations left out: a hoe on rooted dirt (it drops hanging roots from a loot table) and an axe on copper doors and copper chests (both halves change together); the axe's scrape and wax-off particles are not drawn, and the new block skips `updateFromNeighbourShapes`.
- A shovel douses a campfire only where the player may build: adventure players, whom vanilla lets douse, can't.
- The enchanting slot takes tools, armour and books only. The anvil name field only edits at the end.
- Ice whose support breaks in the same frame leaves no water, and melted ice shows air for about a server tick before the water appears.
- There are no advancements, statistics or sculk, so their triggers and game events (eyes of ender, elytra) do nothing.
- To check in game: whether breaking one bed half removes the other, and whether a ladder drops when its wall is removed. The update rules for both exist now.

### Mobs
- Only the kinds listed under [World simulation](#world-simulation-integrated-server-thread) are simulated. The others (piglins, hoglins, striders, foxes, rabbits, goats and the rest) stay dormant: they are kept and saved, count toward the spawn caps and despawn like vanilla, but don't move.

## Testing workflow

The build rules (cargo through `scripts\cargo-slot.ps1`, one target dir per worktree, the track runner and the merge procedure) are in [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md). This section is the per-change checklist.

### Environment
- Run cargo only from PowerShell through the wrapper, e.g. `& scripts\cargo-slot.ps1 test -p minecraft_survival --locked`, or with `+target=D:\iw4l-wt\<name>` in a track worktree. It adds `-j 8`, waits for one of two machine-wide build slots, puts cargo on the `Path`, and sets `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` (default `D:\iw4l-target`) and `MINECRAFTOSS_ROOT` (`C:\Games\Mashup\2010-Rust-Rewrite-Mashup\iw4l-artifacts\minecraft-26.3`) when they are not set. No other environment variables are needed: dev and test builds are not incremental and have no debug info (`Cargo.toml`).
- PowerShell drops a bare `--` when calling a script, so quote it: `'--'`.

### Per change
1. **Unit tests** for each touched crate: `& scripts\cargo-slot.ps1 test -p <crate> --locked`.
   - `third_party/minecraftoss` is outside the workspace. Its packages are `minecraftoss-core`, `minecraftoss-world`, `minecraftoss-generator`, `minecraftoss-player` and `minecraftoss-entities`.
   - `minecraftoss-player` tests need two empty stand-in files: `third_party/scenarios/survival/difficulty-reference.csv` and `difficulty-damage-reference.csv`. `scripts\full-suite.ps1` creates them and deletes them afterwards; by hand, create them, run the tests, then delete `third_party/scenarios`.
   - Tests that need the real game data are gated on `MINECRAFTOSS_ROOT`, which the wrapper sets. Every other test uses synthetic fixtures.
   - Known flaky test: `sections::tests::background_full_update_matches_the_synchronous_one`; re-run once when only it fails. `poof_particles::a_puff_rises_and_fades_within_its_lifetime` was flaky until fd8c390b gave it a fixed seed.
2. **Whole game:** `& scripts\cargo-slot.ps1 check -p launcher --locked` before a commit that touches game code.
3. **rustfmt on new files only**, checking the staged content: `git show :<file> | rustfmt --check --edition <ed>`. The output must be empty (it exits 0 even with a diff). Checkouts are CRLF and `rustfmt.toml` wants LF.
   - Edition 2021: `minecraft_terrain`, `minecraftoss-entities`, `minecraftoss-player`.
   - Edition 2024: everything else.
   - `minecraft_terrain` is edition 2021, so it can't use let-chains.
4. **Commit:** stage only the change's files and end the message with the Co-Authored-By line.

### Per track
1. **Clippy** on the touched crates: `& scripts\cargo-slot.ps1 clippy -p <crate> -p <crate> --locked --all-targets '--' -A clippy::approx_constant`.
   - It must report no warnings on lines the track touched; older warnings stay.
   - The workspace denies `unwrap_or_default`.
2. **The full suite** after the track is merged: `scripts\full-suite.ps1 +target=<the track's target dir>`. It runs the workspace tests without doc-tests, then the five minecraftoss crates with their stand-in files, and takes both build slots. 2086 tests passed at the phase 4 merge of round 7.

### Play test
1. Build `& scripts\cargo-slot.ps1 build --release --locked -p launcher` (fat LTO, about 17 minutes clean and 13 after an edit) into `D:\iw4l-target\release\iw4l.exe`. A mid-round test build may use `--profile play` (about 10 minutes clean, seconds after an edit, into `D:\iw4l-target\play\iw4l.exe`); the last build of a round is always `--release`.
2. Copy it to `C:\Games\Mashup\2010-Rust-Rewrite-Mashup\iw4l-survival.exe`. Older builds are kept as `iw4l-survival-<hash>.exe`.
3. The `.env` next to the exe holds `IW4L_MINECRAFT_KIT=`.
   - Format: a comma list of `id` or `id*count`; the `minecraft:` prefix is optional. `+enchantment` or `+enchantment=level` after the id enchants the item, e.g. `diamond_pickaxe+silk_touch`, and `+damage=N` wears it down; `@potion` fills a potion, splash or lingering potion or tipped arrow (`splash_potion@poison`), and `@dye` dyes leather armour (`leather_chestplate@red`). A count goes last, as in `iron_sword+sharpness=5*2`.
   - It fills only the 27 backpack slots, so give at most 27 stacks. The log prints how many stacks were given. It only goes to a player with no saved inventory in that world, so a test that needs it uses a new `IW4L_MINECRAFT_WORLD`, or `/kit` in game.
4. Start with `iw4l-survival.exe map minecraft:overworld`.
5. The owner plays and sends numbered screenshots in a folder named after the build, e.g. `screenshots-<hash>/` or `ac0c791/`. These folders are git-ignored.
6. Logs: `C:\Games\Mashup\2010-Rust-Rewrite-Mashup\iw4l-artifacts\logs\latest.log`.

| `IW4L_MINECRAFT_GAMEMODE` | Game mode to switch to on world load, over the saved one: `survival`, `creative`, `adventure` or `spectator` (or vanilla's ids 0 to 3) |
| `IW4L_MINECRAFT_DIMENSION` | Starts the player in `overworld`, `the_nether` or `the_end` (with or without `minecraft:`), over the saved dimension: at the saved place when it is in that dimension, else in the Nether through a portal found or built at the scaled place where the player stood (vanilla's not-in-a-portal arrival), or on the End platform |
| `MINECRAFTOSS_DEBUG_SERVER=1` | Logs server changes and chest placement |
| `MINECRAFTOSS_MESH_CHECK=1` | Checks baked sections against unbaked ones |
| `MINECRAFTOSS_GEN_THREADS`, `MINECRAFTOSS_SECTION_THREADS` | Thread counts for world generation and section building |
